### Part I: The Human Eye — Structure, Function & Accommodation (Questions 1–35)

#### 1. On which structure of the human eye is an optical image formed?
A. Cornea  
B. Iris  
C. Pupil  
D. Retina  
**Answer: D**  
*Rationale:* The retina acts as a light-sensitive screen containing millions of photoreceptor cells where real, inverted images are focused by the eye's lens system.

---

#### 2. What are the intrinsic optical characteristics of the image formed by the crystalline eye lens on the retina?
A. Virtual and erect  
B. Real and inverted  
C. Real and erect  
D. Virtual and magnified  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The eye lens is a converging convex lens system that forms a real, inverted, and diminished image on the retina.

---

#### 3. Through which thin membrane does light first enter the human eye?
A. Crystalline lens  
B. Retina  
C. Cornea  
D. Sclera  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* Light enters through the cornea, a thin, transparent spherical bulge on the anterior surface of the eyeball.

---

#### 4. Where does the majority of the refraction of light rays entering the human eye take place?
A. At the anterior surface of the crystalline lens  
B. At the outer surface of the cornea  
C. Inside the vitreous humor  
D. At the fovea centralis of the retina  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Because of the large refractive index difference between air ($n \approx 1.0$) and the cornea/aqueous humor ($n \approx 1.376$), roughly $70\text{–}80\%$ of total light convergence occurs at the outer surface of the cornea.

---

#### 5. What is the approximate anatomical diameter of a normal adult human eyeball?
A. $1.2\text{ cm}$  
B. $2.3\text{ cm}$  
C. $3.5\text{ cm}$  
D. $4.8\text{ cm}$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The human eyeball is nearly spherical with an average anteroposterior diameter of approximately $2.3\text{ cm}$.

---

#### 6. What is the primary optical function of the crystalline lens in the human eye?
A. Providing coarse preliminary refraction of incoming light  
B. Providing fine-tuning of focal length to focus objects at varying distances onto the retina  
C. Preventing total internal reflection of incoming light rays  
D. Completely absorbing harmful infrared radiation  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The crystalline lens provides dynamic, adjustable focus (fine adjustment of curvature) to clearly resolve objects located between the near point and infinity.

---

#### 7. Which muscular structure controls the aperture diameter of the pupil?
A. Ciliary muscles  
B. Iris  
C. Optic nerve fibers  
D. Suspensory ligaments  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The iris is a dark muscular diaphragm behind the cornea that regulates the size of the central pupil opening.

---

#### 8. What is the functional role of the pupil in the human visual system?
A. Generating nerve impulses for transmission to the brain  
B. Regulating and controlling the quantity of light entering the eye  
C. Altering the chemical composition of the vitreous humor  
D. Anchoring the crystalline lens to the sclera  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The pupil expands in low-light environments to admit more light and constricts in bright light to protect the retina and prevent glare.

---

#### 9. What occurs at the cellular level within the retina upon photo-illumination?
A. Photoreceptor cells generate chemical acids that dissolve the sclera  
B. Light-sensitive cells become activated and generate electrical signals  
C. Rhodopsin crystallizes into opaque mineral granules  
D. The retinal basement membrane undergoes cellular mitosis  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Illumination of retinal rods and cones triggers photochemical changes, generating electrical potentials transmitted to the brain via the optic nerve.

---

#### 10. How are electrical impulses generated by the retina transmitted to the brain?
A. Through the lacrimal canal  
B. Via the optic nerve  
C. Through the carotid artery  
D. Via the vitreous membrane  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Axons of retinal ganglion cells bundle together to form the optic nerve, which carries electrical signals directly to the visual cortex.

---

#### 11. What is the physiological property termed the "power of accommodation"?
A. The capacity of the iris to alter pupillary diameter  
B. The ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length via ciliary muscle action  
C. The ability of the retina to regenerate rhodopsin in complete darkness  
D. The corneal resilience against external mechanical trauma  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Accommodation is the dynamic process by which the ciliary muscles alter lens curvature, changing its focal length to maintain sharp focus on objects at different distances.

---

#### 12. When the ciliary muscles are completely relaxed, how do the eye lens and its focal length change?
A. The lens becomes thick, and its focal length decreases  
B. The lens becomes thin, and its focal length increases  
C. The lens becomes spherical, and its focal length reaches zero  
D. The lens shifts anteriorly without altering curvature  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* When ciliary muscles relax, suspensory zonules pull taut, flattening the lens. This makes it thinner, increasing its radius of curvature and focal length to focus on distant objects.

---

#### 13. When viewing an object close to the eye, what coordinated actions occur in the ciliary muscles and eye lens?
A. Ciliary muscles relax; lens flattens; focal length increases  
B. Ciliary muscles contract; lens becomes thicker; focal length decreases  
C. Ciliary muscles contract; lens becomes thinner; focal length decreases  
D. Ciliary muscles relax; lens becomes thicker; focal length increases  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Focusing on nearby objects requires ciliary muscle contraction, which releases tension on suspensory ligaments. The elastic lens rounds up, increasing its curvature and decreasing focal length.

---

#### 14. What is the least distance of distinct vision (near point) for a young adult with normal vision?
A. $2.5\text{ cm}$  
B. $25\text{ cm}$  
C. $2.5\text{ m}$  
D. $\infty$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* For a healthy young adult eye, the closest distance at which an object can be seen comfortably and distinctly without muscular strain is $25\text{ cm}$.

---

#### 15. What is the far point of a normal, healthy human eye?
A. $25\text{ cm}$  
B. $100\text{ m}$  
C. $1\text{ km}$  
D. Infinity ($\infty$)  
**Answer: D**  
*Rationale:* In a fully relaxed, emmetropic eye, parallel rays from infinity are focused directly onto the retina.

---

#### 16. What visual range can be sharply focused by a normal human eye?
A. Between $0\text{ cm}$ and $25\text{ cm}$  
B. Between $25\text{ cm}$ and infinity ($\infty$)  
C. Between $10\text{ cm}$ and $100\text{ m}$  
D. Exclusively at optical infinity  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* A normal eye accommodates seamlessly for all object distances located between its near point ($25\text{ cm}$) and its far point ($\infty$).

---

#### 17. Why does holding a printed book $5\text{ cm}$ away from the eyes cause blurred vision and eye strain?
A. Atmospheric refraction bends light away from the cornea  
B. The focal length of the crystalline lens cannot be reduced below a certain minimum limit  
C. The pupil completely shuts down in response to close objects  
D. The retina runs out of light-sensitive photoreceptor cells  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The ciliary muscles cannot compress the crystalline lens beyond its maximum physiological curvature, setting a lower limit on focal length and causing blur at distances closer than $25\text{ cm}$.

---

#### 18. What optical condition occurs when the crystalline lens of an elderly individual turns milky and cloudy?
A. Presbyopia  
B. Glaucoma  
C. Cataract  
D. Astigmatism  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* Cataract is an age-related condition in which lens proteins denature, clouding the crystalline lens and causing partial or complete loss of transparency.

---

#### 19. How can useful vision be restored in an eye affected by an advanced cataract?
A. Prescribing high-power concave eyeglasses  
B. Surgical removal of the opaque lens, typically replacing it with an artificial intraocular lens  
C. Instilling miotic eye drops to permanently constrict the pupil  
D. Daily exposure to concentrated ultraviolet light  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Cataract involves physical opacification of the lens material. It cannot be corrected with spectacles and requires surgical removal and replacement with an intraocular lens.

---

#### 20. When an object is moved from $5\text{ m}$ to $50\text{ m}$ away from the eye, how does the image distance inside the eye change?
A. It increases by $5\text{ cm}$  
B. It decreases by half  
C. It remains constant (fixed at the distance between lens and retina)  
D. It fluctuates continuously  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* The distance from the crystalline lens to the retinal screen is anatomically fixed at roughly $2.3\text{ cm}$. The eye alters its focal length, not its image distance ($v$).

---

#### 21. What biochemical and mechanical material forms the crystalline lens of the human eye?
A. Dense crystalline silicon dioxide  
B. A transparent, fibrous, jelly-like proteinaceous material  
C. Rigid cellulose polymer fibers  
D. Solid calcified hydroxyapatite  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The crystalline lens is a flexible, transparent structure made of specialized elongated fiber cells filled with water-soluble crystallin proteins.

---

#### 22. What happens to the focal length of the eye lens when an observer looks up from reading a desk notebook to view a distant mountain?
A. The focal length decreases  
B. The focal length increases  
C. The focal length drops to zero  
D. The focal length becomes negative  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Viewing a distant mountain relaxes the ciliary muscles, thinning the lens and increasing its focal length to roughly match the eyeball diameter ($2.3\text{ cm}$).

---

#### 23. Why is it impossible for a human to perceive colors when both eyes are tightly closed in a dark room?
A. The optic nerve only conducts sound signals in the dark  
B. Color perception requires wavelength-dependent activation of retinal photoreceptors by incoming light photons  
C. The brain shuts off all visual cortex processing when eyelids shut  
D. The cornea turns completely opaque without ambient light  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Color vision is mediated by cone photoreceptors, which require sufficient incoming light within the visible spectrum to generate distinguishing electrical signals.

---

#### 24. What happens to the pupil when a person steps from an unlit cinema hall into bright afternoon sunlight?
A. The pupil rapidly dilates due to ciliary contraction  
B. The pupil rapidly constricts due to circular iris muscle contraction  
C. The pupil turns completely white to reflect photons  
D. The pupil remains identical in size  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Bright light triggers a parasympathetic pupillary reflex: circular fibers of the iris contract, reducing the pupil aperture to limit retinal light exposure.

---

#### 25. Which refractory medium lies between the posterior surface of the cornea and the anterior surface of the crystalline lens?
A. Vitreous humor  
B. Aqueous humor  
C. Cerebrospinal fluid  
D. Synovial fluid  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The anterior chamber between the cornea and the lens is filled with aqueous humor, a watery fluid that maintains intraocular pressure and nourishes the cornea.

---

#### 26. Which viscous, jelly-like fluid fills the large posterior cavity of the eyeball between the crystalline lens and the retina?
A. Aqueous humor  
B. Vitreous humor  
C. Blood plasma  
D. Endolymph  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The vitreous humor is a transparent, gelatinous matrix filling the posterior segment, helping preserve the spherical shape of the eyeball.

---

#### 27. What is the focal length of the eye lens when focusing on an object at the normal near point ($25\text{ cm}$), assuming an eyeball diameter of $2.3\text{ cm}$?
A. $+2.30\text{ cm}$  
B. $+2.10\text{ cm}$  
C. $+2.50\text{ cm}$  
D. $+1.80\text{ cm}$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Given $u = -25\text{ cm}$ and $v = +2.3\text{ cm}$:  
$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{2.3} - \frac{1}{-25} = \frac{1}{2.3} + \frac{1}{25} \approx 0.4348 + 0.0400 = 0.4748\text{ cm}^{-1} \implies f \approx 2.11\text{ cm}$$

---

#### 28. What is the focal length of the eye lens when focusing on an object at optical infinity, assuming an eyeball diameter of $2.3\text{ cm}$?
A. $+2.50\text{ cm}$  
B. $+2.30\text{ cm}$  
C. $+2.10\text{ cm}$  
D. $+1.15\text{ cm}$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* For $u = -\infty$:  
$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{-\infty} = \frac{1}{v} - 0 \implies f = v = +2.3\text{ cm}$$

---

#### 29. What is the approximate change in optical power of the eye lens as it accommodates from infinity down to $25\text{ cm}$?
A. $+1.0\text{ D}$  
B. $+4.0\text{ D}$  
C. $+10.0\text{ D}$  
D. $+40.0\text{ D}$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The added accommodation power required to bring an object from infinity to $25\text{ cm}$ ($0.25\text{ m}$) is $\Delta P = \frac{1}{0.25\text{ m}} = +4.0\text{ D}$.

#### 30. How does the brain process the inverted retinal image so that we perceive objects right-side up?
A. Optical fibers physically twist $180^\circ$ through the bony orbit  
B. The crystalline lens reinverts the image before light strikes the optic disk  
C. The visual cortex interprets electrical impulses and post-processes the orientation  
D. Saccadic movements flip the retina upside down twice per second  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* Although the physical optics of the eye create an inverted real image on the retina, the visual processing pathways of the brain interpret these neural inputs to present an upright perceptual view.

---

#### 31. What anatomical feature of the cornea gives it its primary optical converging power?
A. Its dense pigmentation pattern  
B. Its transparent curved spherical surface exposed to ambient air  
C. Its contractile circular muscle fibers  
D. Its connection to the optic chiasm  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The pronounced curved geometry and the refractive index difference between air ($n = 1.0$) and corneal tissue ($n \approx 1.376$) give the cornea its substantial converging ability.

---

#### 32. Which of the following conditions would prevent the ciliary muscles from altering the curvature of the crystalline lens?
A. Loss of elasticity of the crystalline lens tissue  
B. Mild dilation of the pupil  
C. Excessive production of retinal rhodopsin  
D. Exposure to red danger signal lights  
**Answer: A**  
*Rationale:* If the lens loses its structural flexibility (as occurs with advancing age), ciliary contraction can no longer alter its curvature, limiting accommodation.

#### 33. Why does an object placed closer than $25\text{ cm}$ feel strained to look at, even if an observer can force it into momentary focus?

**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Prolonged, extreme contraction of the ciliary muscles to maximize lens thickness strains the muscular tissue, leading to asthenopia (eye strain).

#### 34. What optical term describes an eye that focuses parallel rays from infinity directly onto the retina without accommodation?
A. Myopic eye  
B. Emmetropic (normal) eye  
C. Hypermetropic eye

**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* An emmetropic eye has normal optical dimensions; with ciliary muscles fully relaxed, parallel incident rays focus sharply on the retina.

#### 35. Can a person who has previously undergone successful cataract surgery later donate their eyes after death?

### Part II: Eye Donation Facts & Medical Principles (Questions 36–45)

#### 36. Within how many hours after death must eyes be removed to remain viable for corneal transplantation?

C. Within $24\text{–}48\text{ hours}$  
D. Within $72\text{ hours}$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Corneal tissues begin deteriorating due to lack of oxygen and metabolic breakdown after death; enucleation must occur within 4 to 6 hours.

---

#### 37. How many corneal-blind individuals can receive sight from a single pair of donated eyes?
A. Only one person  
B. Up to four people  
C. Exactly ten people  
D. Up to twelve people  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Advanced surgical split-grafting techniques allow tissues from a single pair of donated eyes to restore sight to up to four corneal-blind patients.

---

#### 38. Which medical condition permanently disqualifies an individual from donating eyes?
A. Diabetes mellitus  
B. Hypertension  
C. Systemic infectious rabies  
D. Controlled bronchial asthma  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* Transmissible pathogens like rabies, HIV/AIDS, viral hepatitis B/C, encephalitis, and tetanus disqualify a donor to prevent transmission to the recipient.

#### 39. Can individuals who wear spectacles for myopia or hypermetropia donate their eyes?

*Rationale:* Common refractive errors relate to eyeball axial length or internal crystalline lens power; corneal tissue remains healthy and suitable for grafting.

#### 41. Which systemic disease listed below does NOT disqualify a deceased person from eye donation?

C. Asthma  
D. Encephalitis  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* Non-communicable chronic conditions like asthma, hypertension, and diabetes do not compromise the safety of corneal transplants.

### Part III: Defects of Vision & Optical Corrections (Questions 46–80)

#### 46. What is the clinical name for near-sightedness?
A. Hypermetropia  
B. Myopia  
C. Presbyopia  
D. Astigmatism  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Myopia is commonly called near-sightedness: near objects are seen clearly, but distant objects appear blurred.

---

#### 47. Where does a myopic eye focus light rays originating from a distant object?
A. Directly on the retinal surface  
B. Behind the retina  
C. In front of the retina  
D. Inside the crystalline lens  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* In myopia, the eye's refractive power is too strong for its axial length, focusing parallel rays in front of the retina.

---

#### 48. Which two anatomical factors can cause myopia?
A. Eyeball too short; crystalline lens curvature too flat  
B. Excessive curvature of the eye lens; elongation of the eyeball  
C. Weakening of ciliary muscles; hardening of the lens  
D. Clouding of the vitreous humor; flattening of the cornea  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Myopia arises when the cornea/lens has excessive converging power or when the eyeball's anteroposterior axis is abnormally elongated.

---

#### 49. What type of corrective lens restores clear distance vision to a myopic individual?
A. Cylindrical convex lens  
B. Concave (diverging) lens of suitable power  
C. Convex (converging) lens of suitable power  
D. Bifocal lens with an upper convex segment  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* A concave lens diverges incoming parallel rays so that they appear to emerge from the myopic eye's actual far point, focusing the final image on the retina.

---

#### 50. What is the location of the far point for a person suffering from myopia?
A. At optical infinity ($\infty$)  
B. Closer than infinity (at a finite distance of a few meters or centimeters)  
C. At $25\text{ cm}$ exactly  
D. Behind the occipital lobe  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* A myopic eye cannot resolve parallel rays from infinity; its far point shifts inward to a finite distance in front of the eye.

---

#### 51. What is the clinical name for far-sightedness?
A. Myopia  
B. Hypermetropia  
C. Presbyopia  
D. Cataract  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Hypermetropia is far-sightedness: distant objects can be focused, but nearby objects appear blurred.

---

#### 52. Where are light rays from an object at the normal near point ($25\text{ cm}$) focused in an uncorrected hypermetropic eye?
A. In front of the retina  
B. At a focal point behind the retina  
C. Directly on the corneal surface  
D. Uniformly inside the anterior chamber  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Because the hypermetropic eye lacks sufficient converging power, rays from nearby objects reach the retina before converging, forming a focal point behind it.

---

#### 53. Which two structural factors can cause hypermetropia?
A. Eyeball elongation; excessive corneal curvature  
B. Focal length of the eye lens is too long; eyeball is too short  
C. Clouding of the lens capsule; detachment of the retina  
D. Ciliary muscle hypertrophy; enlargement of the pupil  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Hypermetropia occurs when the converging power of the eye is too weak (focal length too long) or the eyeball is too short along its optical axis.

---

#### 54. What type of corrective lens is used to restore comfortable reading vision in hypermetropia?
A. Concave lens  
B. Convex (converging) lens of appropriate power  
C. Simple flat glass plate  
D. Plano-concave diverging lens  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* A convex lens provides supplementary convergence, bending diverging rays from $25\text{ cm}$ so they appear to originate from the person's receded near point.

---

#### 55. Where is the near point situated for an individual suffering from hypermetropia?
A. Closer than $25\text{ cm}$ (e.g., at $10\text{ cm}$)  
B. Farther away than the normal near point ($> 25\text{ cm}$)  
C. At optical infinity  
D. Exactly at the corneal surface  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Because the hypermetropic eye cannot provide sufficient refractive power for close objects, its near point recedes beyond $25\text{ cm}$ (often to $50\text{ cm}\text{–}1\text{ m}$).

---

#### 56. What is the age-related visual defect termed presbyopia?
A. Opacification of the crystalline lens matrix  
B. Gradual reduction in accommodation power due to aging  
C. Pigment accumulation in the vitreous humor  
D. Progressive shortening of retinal rod outer segments  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Presbyopia is the normal aging process wherein the crystalline lens loses elasticity and the ciliary muscles weaken, diminishing accommodation.

---

#### 57. What are the primary underlying causes of presbyopia?
A. Bacterial infection of the corneal stroma  
B. Gradual weakening of ciliary muscles and diminishing flexibility of the crystalline eye lens  
C. Elongation of the optic nerve canal  
D. Shrinkage of the anterior chamber volume  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Aging reduces both ciliary muscle contractile force and crystalline lens compliance, causing the near point to recede.

---

#### 58. What type of spectacles is commonly prescribed for an elderly person experiencing both myopia and hypermetropia?
A. Toric contact lenses  
B. Bifocal lenses  
C. Simple tinted sunglasses  
D. High-power plano-concave lenses  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Individuals with both distant and near vision deficits benefit from bifocal lenses containing two distinct optical segments.

---

#### 59. In a standard bifocal spectacle lens, what are the lens types in the upper and lower segments, and what are their functions?
A. Upper: convex (near vision); Lower: concave (distant vision)  
B. Upper: concave (distant vision); Lower: convex (near vision)  
C. Upper: cylindrical; Lower: plane glass  
D. Upper: opaque shield; Lower: tinted filter  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The upper segment features a concave lens to correct distant vision along the primary line of sight. The lower segment features a convex lens to support near vision when looking downward to read.

---

#### 60. A student seated in the back row of a classroom cannot read the blackboard clearly, but reads their desktop textbook without difficulty. What is the defect and its correction?
A. Hypermetropia; corrected using a convex lens  
B. Myopia; corrected using a concave lens  
C. Presbyopia; corrected using a bifocal lens  
D. Cataract; corrected via surgical irrigation  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Inability to resolve distant writing (blackboard) combined with clear near vision (textbook) is classic myopia, corrected with diverging (concave) lenses.

---

#### 61. An adult holds reading material at arm's length ($75\text{ cm}$) to see the print clearly. What is the defect and its correction?
A. Myopia; concave lens  
B. Hypermetropia; convex lens  
C. Astigmatism; cylindrical lens  
D. Nyctalopia; vitamin A supplementation  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* A receded near point ($75\text{ cm} > 25\text{ cm}$) alongside intact distant vision indicates hypermetropia, which requires converging (convex) lenses.

---

#### 62. What sign convention is used for the focal lengths of corrective lenses for myopia and hypermetropia?
A. Myopia: positive ($+$); Hypermetropia: negative ($-$)  
B. Myopia: negative ($-$) (concave); Hypermetropia: positive ($+$) (convex)  
C. Both prescriptions carry positive signs  
D. Both prescriptions carry negative signs  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Concave lenses have negative focal lengths and powers ($P < 0$), whereas convex lenses have positive focal lengths and powers ($P > 0$).

#### 63. A person requires a lens of power $-5.5\text{ D}$ for distant vision. What is the focal length of this lens?
A. $-0.182\text{ m}$ (or $-18.2\text{ cm}$)

C. $-5.50\text{ m}$  
D. $-0.55\text{ m}$  
**Answer: A**  
*Rationale:*  
$$f = \frac{1}{P} = \frac{1}{-5.5\text{ D}} = -0.1818\text{ m} \approx -18.2\text{ cm}$$

#### 64. A person requires a lens of power $+1.5\text{ D}$ for correcting near vision. What is the focal length of this lens?
A. $+1.50\text{ m}$  
B. $+0.667\text{ m}$ (or $+66.7\text{ cm}$)  
C. $-0.667\text{ m}$  
D. $+0.150\text{ m}$

*Rationale:*  
$$f = \frac{1}{P} = \frac{1}{+1.5\text{ D}} = +\frac{2}{3}\text{ m} \approx +0.667\text{ m} = +66.7\text{ cm}$$

#### 65. The far point of a myopic person is $80\text{ cm}$ in front of the eye. What is the nature and power of the corrective lens required?
A. Convex lens, $+1.25\text{ D}$  
B. Concave lens, $-1.25\text{ D}$  
C. Concave lens, $-0.80\text{ D}$  
D. Convex lens, $+0.80\text{ D}$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* To see distant objects clearly, an object at $u = -\infty$ must form a virtual image at the far point $v = -80\text{ cm} = -0.80\text{ m}$:

$$P = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-0.80\text{ m}} = -1.25\text{ D}\quad (\text{Concave lens})$$

### Part IV: Refraction Through a Triangular Glass Prism (Questions 81–95)

#### 81. How many triangular bases and rectangular lateral surfaces comprise a standard triangular glass prism?

B. Two triangular bases and three rectangular lateral surfaces  
C. Four triangular surfaces and one square base  
D. Six rectangular surfaces  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* A triangular prism consists of two parallel triangular bases joined by three rectangular lateral refracting faces.

#### 82. What is the angle between the two lateral refracting faces of a triangular prism called?
A. Angle of deviation ($\angle D$)  
B. Angle of emergence ($\angle e$)  
C. Angle of the prism ($\angle A$)  
D. Critical angle ($\angle C$)  
**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* The dihedral angle between the two lateral faces where refraction occurs is defined as the angle of the prism ($\angle A$).

#### 83. When a ray of light enters from air into a glass prism at its first face, how does it bend relative to the normal?
A. It bends away from the normal  
B. It bends towards the normal  
C. It continues straight without deviation  
D. It undergoes specular $180^\circ$ reflection  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Glass is optically denser than air ($n_{\text{glass}} > n_{\text{air}}$). As light slows upon entering the glass, it bends toward the normal.

#### 84. When the refracted ray inside a glass prism exits through the second face into air, how does it bend?

C. It re-enters along the incident trajectory  
D. It is completely absorbed at the exit boundary  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Exiting from an optically denser medium (glass) into an optically rarer medium (air) causes the ray to speed up and bend away from the normal, deflecting toward the thicker base of the prism.

#### 85. What is the angle of deviation ($\angle D$) in the refraction of light through a prism?
A. The angle between the incident ray and the normal to the first surface  
B. The angle between the original direction of the incident ray and the emergent ray  
C. The angle between the two triangular bases  
D. The sum of the two internal angles of refraction  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* The angle of deviation ($\angle D$) is the angular difference between the projected path of the incident ray and the direction of the emergent ray.

#### 86. For refraction through a triangular prism, what mathematical relation links $\angle i$, $\angle e$, $\angle A$, and $\angle D$?

C. $\angle i - \angle e = \angle A - \angle D$  
D. $\angle i \times \angle e = \angle A \times \angle D$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Geometry dictates that the sum of the incident angle ($\angle i$) and emergent angle ($\angle e$) equals the sum of the prism angle ($\angle A$) and deviation angle ($\angle D$).

---

#### 87. In a glass prism experiment, a ray is incident at an angle of $48^\circ$ on a prism with an apex angle $A = 60^\circ$. If the emergent angle is $42^\circ$, what is the angle of deviation?

B. $30^\circ$  
C. $60^\circ$  
D. $45^\circ$  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:*  
$$\angle i + \angle e = \angle A + \angle D \implies 48^\circ + 42^\circ = 60^\circ + \angle D \implies 90^\circ = 60^\circ + \angle D \implies \angle D = 30^\circ$$

#### 88. Why does an emergent ray from a triangular glass prism deviate from its incident direction, unlike in a parallel-sided glass slab?

C. Prisms absorb all transverse electric fields  
D. Prisms reflect light purely by total internal reflection  
**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* In a rectangular slab with parallel faces, the bends at both surfaces cancel out ($i = e$), leaving the emergent ray parallel to the incident ray. In a prism, the inclined faces produce non-parallel surface normals, causing cumulative deviation.

D. $90^\circ$  
**Answer: A**  
*Rationale:* By Snell's law, when light strikes a surface at normal incidence ($i = 0^\circ$), the angle of refraction is also zero ($r_1 = 0^\circ$).

#### 90. In Question 89, what will be the angle of incidence ($r_2$) at the second face of the equilateral prism ($A = 60^\circ$)?

**Answer: C**  
*Rationale:* For any prism, $r_1 + r_2 = A$. With $r_1 = 0^\circ$ and $A = 60^\circ$:

### Part VIII: Scattering of Light & Real-World Optical Colors (Questions 136–150)

#### 136. What optical phenomenon occurs when light is redirected in all directions by collisions with fine particles suspended in a medium?

**Answer: B**  
*Rationale:* Scattering is the process by which particles absorb and re-radiate incident light in various directions.

#### 137. Why is the path of a beam of light invisible in a true solution, yet clearly visible in a colloidal solution?

**Answer: B**

#### 141. How does the wavelength of scattered light depend on the physical size of the scattering particles?
