**Directions:** In each of the following 50 questions, two statements are given—one labeled **Assertion (A)** and the other labeled **Reason (R)**. Select the correct option from the choices given below:

* **(a)** Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
* **(b)** Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
* **(c)** (A) is true, but (R) is false.
* **(d)** (A) is false, but (R) is true.

### Section 1: Anatomy of the Eye & Accommodation (Q1–Q12)

**1.**
**Assertion (A):** The majority of the refraction of light entering the human eye takes place at the outer surface of the cornea.

**Reason (R):** The refractive index difference between air and the corneal surface is much larger than the difference between the crystalline lens and its surrounding fluids.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** The cornea forms a convex transparent boundary with air. Because the refractive index jump from air ($n \approx 1.00$) to the cornea ($n \approx 1.376$) is the steepest optical transition in the ocular path, nearly 75–80% of total convergence occurs right here. The crystalline lens provides only the finer dynamic adjustments.

**2.**
**Assertion (A):** When an object is moved closer to the eye, the image distance inside the eye decreases to keep the image focused.

**Reason (R):** According to the thin lens formula $\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$, as $u$ decreases in magnitude, $v$ must change if $f$ remains constant.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** In the human eye, the image distance $v$ (distance from lens system to retina) is strictly fixed by eyeball anatomy ($\approx 2.3\text{ cm}$). When an object comes closer, the ciliary muscles contract to increase lens curvature, thereby decreasing focal length $f$ so that $v$ remains absolutely constant.

**3.**
**Assertion (A):** When looking at a nearby object, the ciliary muscles contract, causing the eye lens to become thicker.

**Reason (R):** Contraction of the ciliary muscles increases the tension on the suspensory ligaments holding the eye lens.

**Answer:** **(c)**

**Explanation:** When ciliary muscles contract, the muscular ring tightens inward, which releases (relaxes) tension on the suspensory zonules/ligaments. The natural elasticity of the lens then allows it to round up, becoming thicker and more curved (shorter focal length).

**4.**
**Assertion (A):** A normal human eye cannot focus distinctly on an object placed at a distance of $10\text{ cm}$ from the eye without blurriness and severe strain.

**Reason (R):** The focal length of the crystalline eye lens cannot be decreased below a certain structural minimum limit.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** The ciliary muscles can only contract up to a physiological limit. Once the eye reaches its least distance of distinct vision ($\approx 25\text{ cm}$ for a young adult), further decrease in focal length is impossible, preventing the rays from converging sharply onto the retina.

**5.**
**Assertion (A):** The pupil of the eye appears black.

**Reason (R):** No light is reflected back from inside the eye through the pupil under normal ambient conditions.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** The pupil is an aperture in the iris. Light entering the pupil is absorbed by the pigmented choroid layer and retinal tissues inside the eyeball, meaning virtually zero incident light escapes back to the observer, making the aperture look jet black.

**6.**
**Assertion (A):** On entering a dark cinema hall from bright sunlight, a person takes some time to see the surroundings clearly.

**Reason (R):** The iris takes time to contract the pupil to allow more light to enter the eye.

**Answer:** **(c)**

**Explanation:** The iris dilates (expands) the pupil in dim light to let in more light; it contracts the pupil in bright light. Thus, the reasoning statement asserting that the iris "contracts the pupil to allow more light" is false.

**7.**
**Assertion (A):** The image formed on the human retina is real and inverted, yet we perceive objects erect.

**Reason (R):** The electrical impulses generated by retinal photoreceptors are processed and interpreted right-side-up by the visual cortex of the brain.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** The eye's converging optical system inherently casts an inverted real image onto the concave retinal surface. The optic nerve carries these signals to the brain, which flips and interprets the visual data in an upright spatial orientation.

**8.**
**Assertion (A):** Cataract can be corrected by using high-power converging spectacles.

**Reason (R):** Cataract develops when the crystalline lens becomes cloudy and milky due to protein aggregation at old age.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** Cataract causes optical opacity of the lens itself; light cannot pass through properly. Spectacles cannot clear the opaque crystalline matrix. Vision can only be restored through surgical extraction of the cloudy lens and implantation of an artificial intraocular lens (IOL).

**9.**
**Assertion (A):** A person who has undergone cataract surgery cannot donate their eyes after death.

**Reason (R):** Cataract surgery disrupts the cornea and damages the retinal photoreceptors.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** Both statements are false, but under standard marking criteria: cornea transplantation requires only a clear cornea. Cataract surgery replaces the internal lens; the donor’s cornea remains completely viable for transplantation. (A is false; R is also false).

**10.**
**Assertion (A):** Eyes from a deceased donor must be removed within 4 to 6 hours after death.

**Reason (R):** Corneal tissues undergo cellular autolysis and degrade rapidly due to lack of oxygen and metabolic nutrient supply after circulation ceases.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Post-mortem tissue viability decreases swiftly. For successful corneal harvesting and subsequent transplantation, procurement must take place within a 4 to 6-hour window before significant endothelial cell necrosis occurs.

**11.**
**Assertion (A):** When the eye is focused on an object at infinity, the ciliary muscles are in their most relaxed state.

**Reason (R):** In the relaxed state of ciliary muscles, the focal length of the eye lens achieves its maximum value.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Distant vision corresponds to zero muscular strain. The ciliary muscles relax, the suspensory ligaments pull taut, the crystalline lens flattens out into its thinnest shape, and its focal length expands to match the distance to the retina ($\approx 2.3\text{ cm}$).

**12.**
**Assertion (A):** The human eyeball is an optically rigid system that cannot adjust image distance $v$.

**Reason (R):** The diameter of the eyeball is approximately $2.3\text{ cm}$ and remains mechanically invariable during accommodation.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Because the eyeball is fixed at roughly $2.3\text{ cm}$, the image distance cannot be physically moved forward or backward (unlike a camera bellows). Accommodation relies exclusively on modulating lens curvature ($f$).

### Section 2: Defects of Vision & Optical Corrections (Q13–Q24)

**13.**
**Assertion (A):** A myopic person can see nearby objects distinctly, but distant objects appear blurred.

**Reason (R):** In a myopic eye, the image of a distant object is formed behind the retina.

**Answer:** **(c)**

**Explanation:** In myopia (near-sightedness), the refractive power of the eye is excessively strong or the eyeball is elongated; hence, parallel rays from a distant object converge too early and focus **in front of** the retina, not behind it.

**14.**
**Assertion (A):** A concave lens of appropriate focal length is used to correct myopia.

**Reason (R):** A concave lens diverges the parallel rays coming from infinity so that they appear to diverge from the person's defective far point.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** The far point of a myopic eye has shifted from infinity to a finite distance $d$. A concave lens produces a virtual image of a distant object at this defective far point ($v = -d$), allowing the eye lens to focus the rays cleanly onto the retina.

**15.**
**Assertion (A):** A student sitting in the back row has difficulty reading the blackboard, indicating hypermetropia.

**Reason (R):** Hypermetropia is the inability to see distant objects clearly.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** Difficulty viewing the blackboard (a distant object) indicates near-sightedness (myopia), not hypermetropia. Hypermetropia is far-sightedness, where distant vision is fine but near vision is defective.

**16.**
**Assertion (A):** Hypermetropia can be caused by the shortening of the eyeball along its visual axis.

**Reason (R):** If the eyeball is too short, incoming divergent rays from a near object are focused at a virtual point behind the retina.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** When the axial length of the eyeball is abnormally short, the distance between the lens and the retina is insufficient for the converging power of the eye, causing the focal point of near rays to lie behind the retinal plane.

**17.**
**Assertion (A):** The power of a spectacle lens prescribed to correct a hypermetropic eye is always positive.

**Reason (R):** A hypermetropic eye requires an additional converging lens to supplement its deficient converging power.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Because the eye's natural optics fail to converge light sufficiently onto the retina, a convex lens is needed. Convex lenses have a positive focal length, which yields a positive optical power ($P = \frac{1}{f}$).

**18.**
**Assertion (A):** Presbyopia is fundamentally identical to hypermetropia in terms of its biological origin.

**Reason (R):** Both presbyopia and hypermetropia shift the near point of the eye farther away from the standard $25\text{ cm}$.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** While both defects result in a receded near point, their biological origins differ fundamentally. Hypermetropia is a refractive/axial structural defect (short eyeball or intrinsically low power). Presbyopia is an age-related loss of ciliary muscle tone and loss of lens flexibility.

**19.**
**Assertion (A):** Bifocal lenses are prescribed for individuals suffering simultaneously from myopia and hypermetropia/presbyopia.

**Reason (R):** In a standard bifocal lens, the upper section consists of a convex lens for near vision, and the lower section is a concave lens for distant vision.

**Answer:** **(c)**

**Explanation:** The upper section is **concave** (divergent) to assist horizontal, distant viewing. The lower section is **convex** (convergent) because the gaze shifts downward when reading nearby material.

**20.**
**Assertion (A):** A person who can see clearly only up to $1.5\text{ m}$ requires a lens of power $-0.67\text{ D}$ to restore normal distant vision.

**Reason (R):** The corrective focal length for a myopic eye with far point $d$ is given by $f = -d$, and power is $P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (in meters)}}$.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** For an object at infinity ($u = -\infty$) to form an image at the far point ($v = -1.5\text{ m}$), the thin lens formula yields $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-1.5} - \frac{1}{-\infty} = -\frac{1}{1.5}$. Thus, $P = -\frac{1}{1.5} = -0.667\text{ D} \approx -0.67\text{ D}$. Both statements are true and (R) correctly explains (A).

**21.**
**Assertion (A):** An elderly person suffering from presbyopia holds a newspaper at arm's length to read comfortably.

**Reason (R):** The near point of a presbyopic eye recedes to a distance greater than $25\text{ cm}$.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Due to stiffening of the lens and weakened ciliary muscles, the eye cannot achieve the short focal length needed to view objects at $25\text{ cm}$. Pushing the text farther out moves it to or beyond the receded near point where the eye can focus.

**22.**
**Assertion (A):** A hypermetropic person with a near point of $1\text{ m}$ requires a converging lens of power $+3.0\text{ D}$ to read comfortably at $25\text{ cm}$.

**Reason (R):** Applying the lens equation with $u = -0.25\text{ m}$ and $v = -1.0\text{ m}$ gives $f = +0.333\text{ m}$, which corresponds to $+3.0\text{ D}$.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-1} - \frac{1}{-0.25} = -1 + 4 = +3\text{ m}^{-1}$. Hence, $P = +3\text{ D}$. The calculations in both (A) and (R) are mathematically correct and the reasoning directly derives the assertion.

**23.**
**Assertion (A):** Persons who were infected with or died from tetanus or rabies cannot donate their eyes.

**Reason (R):** Pathogens causing systemic transmissible infections pose a serious risk of transmission to the graft recipient.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Standards for corneal harvesting exclude donors with acute infections such as rabies, tetanus, hepatitis B/C, encephalitis, and HIV to prevent pathogen transmission through ocular tissues.

**24.**
**Assertion (A):** The power of accommodation of a normal human eye is approximately $4\text{ D}$.

**Reason (R):** The power of accommodation is the difference between the converging power of the eye when looking at infinity and that when looking at the near point ($25\text{ cm}$).

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** At the far point ($u = \infty$), $P_\infty = \frac{1}{0.023}$. At the near point ($u = 0.25\text{ m}$), additional power required is $\Delta P = \frac{1}{0.25} = +4\text{ D}$. Accommodation amplitude is indeed $\approx 4\text{ D}$.

### Section 3: Refraction Through a Prism (Q25–Q30)

**25.**
**Assertion (A):** A ray of light passing through a triangular glass prism suffers lateral displacement without any angular deviation.

**Reason (R):** The refracting faces of a triangular glass prism are parallel to each other.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** A rectangular glass slab has parallel faces and produces lateral displacement without net deviation. A triangular prism has non-parallel, inclined refracting faces; it produces an **angular deviation** ($\angle D$), bending the emergent ray toward the thicker base.

**26.**
**Assertion (A):** For refraction through a triangular prism, the relation $\angle i + \angle e = \angle A + \angle D$ always holds true.

**Reason (R):** In any quadrilateral formed by the refracting surfaces and normals, the sum of internal angles is $360^\circ$, and in the interior triangle, the sum is $180^\circ$.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Geometrical analysis of the cyclic quadrilateral formed by the normal intersections and prism apex angle $\angle A$, combined with exterior angle properties of the ray deviation triangle, rigorously proves that $\angle i + \angle e = \angle A + \angle D$.

**27.**
**Assertion (A):** The emergent ray from a glass prism always bends towards the base of the prism (assuming the prism is placed in air).

**Reason (R):** Glass has a higher optical density than air, so the ray bends towards the normal at entry and away from the normal at exit.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** At the first inclined face (air to glass), light bends toward the normal. At the second face (glass to air), it bends away from the normal. Because the faces are angled toward an apex, both refractions steer the path downward toward the wider base.

**28.**
**Assertion (A):** The angle of deviation produced by a prism is identical for all wavelengths of light for a given angle of incidence.

**Reason (R):** The angle of deviation depends entirely on the angle of the prism $\angle A$ and the angle of incidence $\angle i$.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** Deviation depends critically on the refractive index ($n$) of the prism material: $\angle D \approx (\mu - 1)\angle A$ for thin prisms. Because refractive index varies with wavelength ($n_{\text{violet}} > n_{\text{red}}$), different colors deviate by different amounts.

**29.**
**Assertion (A):** When a monochromatic beam of light passes through a prism, it undergoes deviation but does not disperse.

**Reason (R):** Dispersion requires a mixture of components with different wavelengths travelling at different speeds in the medium.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Monochromatic light consists of a single wavelength. It experiences refraction and deviation at the faces, but cannot split into multiple colors because it contains no other spectral components.

**30.**
**Assertion (A):** If the angle of a triangular prism is increased, the angle of deviation for a given angle of incidence generally increases.

**Reason (R):** The angle of deviation is directly related to the angle of the prism through the relation $\angle D = \angle i + \angle e - \angle A$.

**Answer:** **(b)**

**Explanation:** As prism angle $\angle A$ increases, the ray encounters greater geometric inclination between surfaces, leading to sharper turns and a larger angle of emergence $\angle e$, which increases net deviation $\angle D$. While both statements are scientifically true, the formula alone does not explain *why* $\angle e$ changes with $\angle A$ without Snell's law.

### Section 4: Dispersion & Rainbow Formation (Q31–Q38)

**31.**
**Assertion (A):** Red light suffers the minimum deviation while violet light suffers the maximum deviation when white light passes through a glass prism.

**Reason (R):** In glass, red light travels faster than violet light, meaning the refractive index of glass is lowest for red and highest for violet.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Refractive index is defined as $n = \frac{c}{v}$. Since $v_{\text{red}} > v_{\text{violet}}$ in a dispersive medium like glass, $n_{\text{red}} < n_{\text{violet}}$. According to Snell's law, a higher refractive index induces sharper bending, causing violet to deviate the most and red the least.

**32.**
**Assertion (A):** Isaac Newton showed that sunlight is composed of seven constituent colors using two identical prisms positioned parallel and inverted relative to one another.

**Reason (R):** The second inverted prism cancels the angular dispersion produced by the first prism, recombining the colors into emergent white light.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** The first prism disperses white light into its spectral band. The second identical prism, placed in an inverted orientation, provides an equal and opposite dispersion effect, bending the rays back together to exit as parallel, recombined white light.

**33.**
**Assertion (A):** A rainbow can be observed when looking directly toward the Sun immediately after a rain shower.

**Reason (R):** Raindrops act as small prisms that reflect light directly forward toward the Sun.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** A rainbow is always formed in the direction **opposite** to that of the Sun. An observer must have the Sun behind their back; incoming sunlight enters raindrops in front of the observer, undergoes internal reflection, and is directed backward toward the observer's eyes.

**34.**
**Assertion (A):** The formation of a rainbow involves refraction, dispersion, and internal reflection of sunlight within atmospheric water droplets.

**Reason (R):** Dispersion of sunlight occurs exclusively at the point of internal reflection on the back wall of the raindrop.

**Answer:** **(c)**

**Explanation:** (A) is true, but (R) is false. Dispersion (the separation of colors) happens primarily at the **first surface** when light enters the droplet from air to water due to wavelength-dependent refraction. The back surface simply reflects the separated rays internally.

**35.**
**Assertion (A):** In a vacuum, all colors of visible light travel with the exact same speed.

**Reason (R):** Vacuum is a non-dispersive medium where the speed of electromagnetic waves depends solely on permittivity $\varepsilon_0$ and permeability $\mu_0$.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** In vacuum, $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}} \approx 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$ for all electromagnetic wavelengths. Dispersion occurs only in material media (glass, water) where light waves interact with matter, making speed frequency-dependent.

**36.**
**Assertion (A):** The acronym VIBGYOR represents the spectrum of white light arranged in order of decreasing wavelength.

**Reason (R):** Violet light has the shortest wavelength ($\approx 400\text{ nm}$) and red light has the longest wavelength ($\approx 700\text{ nm}$) in the visible spectrum.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** VIBGYOR is arranged in order of **increasing** wavelength (Violet is $\approx 400\text{ nm}$, Red is $\approx 700\text{ nm}$). Therefore, Assertion (A) is false, while Reason (R) is factually true.

**37.**
**Assertion (A):** You can observe a rainbow on a sunny day even without rain by looking through a water fountain with the Sun behind you.

**Reason (R):** Suspended water droplets from the fountain refract, disperse, and internally reflect incident sunlight back toward the observer.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Any fine mist or suspension of water droplets in air behaves like miniature prisms. With the Sun behind the observer, rays undergo the same sequence of entry-refraction, internal reflection, and exit-refraction, generating a visible rainbow arc.

**38.**
**Assertion (A):** In the emergent spectrum from a prism resting on its base, red color appears at the top and violet at the bottom.

**Reason (R):** Violet light bends through the greatest angle relative to the incident direction, whereas red light bends the least.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Since the base is at the bottom, bending "downward" toward the base means the color that deviates the most (violet) is deflected furthest downward. The color deviating least (red) remains highest in the emerging fan.

### Section 5: Atmospheric Refraction (Q39–Q44)

**39.**
**Assertion (A):** Stars twinkle when viewed from the Earth's surface, but planets do not.

**Reason (R):** Stars are point-sized light sources whose apparent path fluctuates sharply through turbulent atmospheric layers, while planets act as extended sources that average out intensity fluctuations.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Stars are so far away that they act as optical point sources. Minute shifts in atmospheric density alter their ray paths, causing rapid fluctuations in brightness and position (twinkling). Planets are much closer, subtending larger discs; fluctuations from different points across their surfaces cancel out, yielding steady light.

**40.**
**Assertion (A):** The apparent position of a star in the night sky is slightly higher than its actual physical position when viewed near the horizon.

**Reason (R):** Starlight traveling from space passes into progressively denser atmospheric layers, bending continuously toward the normal.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** As starlight travels through the atmosphere, the refractive index increases toward the surface ($n_{\text{air}} > 1$). The ray constantly bends toward the normal (downward). Tracing the tangent to this curved ray backward into space places the apparent position above the true position.

**41.**
**Assertion (A):** The Sun is visible to us about $2\text{ minutes}$ before actual sunrise and about $2\text{ minutes}$ after actual sunset.

**Reason (R):** Atmospheric refraction bends light rays originating from below the astronomical horizon toward the Earth's surface.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Even when the Sun is physically below the horizon line, rays traversing the curved, stratified atmosphere bend downward toward the observer's eye, allowing the Sun's disc to be seen roughly 2 minutes before it crosses the horizon and 2 minutes after it dips below.

**42.**
**Assertion (A):** The apparent duration of day on Earth is lengthened by approximately $4\text{ minutes}$ due to atmospheric refraction.

**Reason (R):** Advanced sunrise adds roughly $2\text{ minutes}$ and delayed sunset adds roughly $2\text{ minutes}$ to the total daylight period.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Because atmospheric refraction shifts the visible sunrise $2\text{ minutes}$ early and delays the visible sunset by $2\text{ minutes}$, the total period of daylight expands by $2 + 2 = 4\text{ minutes}$.

**43.**
**Assertion (A):** The Sun's disc appears distinctly oval or flattened at sunrise and sunset.

**Reason (R):** The lower edge of the solar disc is closer to the horizon than the upper edge, causing its bottom rays to experience greater atmospheric refraction than its top rays.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Atmospheric density changes rapidly near the horizon. The ray path from the bottom edge of the Sun passes through more refractive air and is lifted upward more than the light from the top edge, compressing the vertical diameter and producing an apparent oval shape.

**44.**
**Assertion (A):** Objects viewed through a turbulent stream of hot air rising above a campfire appear to waver or flicker.

**Reason (R):** Hot air has a slightly lower refractive index than cooler air, and dynamic convection currents cause rapid, chaotic changes in the refractive index profile.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Hot air expands and becomes optically less dense than surrounding cool air. Turbulent convective mixing causes spatial and temporal fluctuations in the refractive index, continuously altering the ray trajectories and creating an unsteady, wavering image.

### Section 6: Scattering of Light & Tyndall Effect (Q45–Q50)

**45.**
**Assertion (A):** The clear sky appears blue during a bright sunny day.

**Reason (R):** Nitrogen and oxygen gas molecules in the atmosphere have physical dimensions much smaller than the wavelength of visible light and scatter blue light far more effectively than red light.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Rayleigh scattering governs scattering by particles smaller than visible wavelengths ($d \ll \lambda$). Scattering intensity varies inversely with the fourth power of wavelength ($I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4}$). Because blue light has roughly half the wavelength of red light, it is scattered about $1.8^4 \approx 10$ to $15$ times more intensely across the sky.

**46.**
**Assertion (A):** The sky appears dark or pitch black to an astronaut orbiting in space or to a passenger flying at very high altitudes.

**Reason (R):** At extreme altitudes and in outer space, the atmosphere is virtually absent, meaning there are no gas molecules or aerosol particles to scatter sunlight toward the eye.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Without particles to scatter light rays in all directions, sunlight travels in straight lines without illuminating the surrounding sky. Unless looking directly at a luminous body, the celestial background appears black.

**47.**
**Assertion (A):** Danger signal lights installed at road intersections and railway crossings are invariably red.

**Reason (R):** Red light has the longest wavelength in the visible spectrum and is least scattered by atmospheric fog, mist, or smoke particles.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** Because red light has the longest wavelength among visible colors, it undergoes minimal scattering ($I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4}$). As a result, its intensity is preserved over long distances through fog or smoke without washing out.

**48.**
**Assertion (A):** Clouds in the lower atmosphere generally appear white rather than blue.

**Reason (R):** Cloud droplets and water aggregates have sizes much larger than the wavelength of visible light, causing all wavelengths of the solar spectrum to scatter almost equally.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** When particle size $a \gg \lambda$, scattering transitions out of the Rayleigh regime into non-selective geometric scattering. All visible wavelengths (red through violet) are scattered with equal efficiency, and their blend is perceived by the human eye as white.

**49.**
**Assertion (A):** The path of a beam of light is clearly visible when passing through a true salt solution.

**Reason (R):** The solute particles in a true solution have diameters greater than $100\text{ nm}$ and scatter incident light efficiently.

**Answer:** **(d)**

**Explanation:** Both statements are false. In a true solution, solute particles are molecularly dispersed ($< 1\text{ nm}$), which is too small to cause visible Tyndall scattering. Hence, the light path remains invisible. The path becomes visible in colloidal solutions and fine suspensions.

**50.**
**Assertion (A):** The Sun appears reddish during sunrise and sunset.

**Reason (R):** Near the horizon, sunlight travels through a thicker layer of atmosphere and a longer distance, scattering away most of the shorter blue wavelengths before the light reaches the observer.

**Answer:** **(a)**

**Explanation:** When the Sun is near the horizon, its rays travel through a much greater optical path length through the atmosphere. The shorter blue and violet wavelengths are heavily scattered out of the direct line of sight, leaving primarily the unscattered, longer red and orange wavelengths to enter the observer's eyes.
